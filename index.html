Sets & reps

Every set in this program is 10 to 14 reps — never fewer than 10, never more than 14. Each exercise has its own target:

Exercise groupSets (intermediate)RepsExamples
Heavy main lifts410Bench press, deadlift, squat, overhead press, Romanian deadlift
Other compound lifts410–12Rows, pull-ups, leg press, incline press, dips
Isolation exercises312–14Curls, pushdowns, flys, leg extensions, leg curls
Small shoulder muscles412–14Lateral raise, face pull, reverse pec deck

Beginners do 3 sets of everything; advanced members add 1 set. Pick a weight you can lift for the lowest rep number with good form — when you reach the highest number on every set, increase the weight.

Starting weights (kg) by level

Every exercise has a starting weight in kilograms for beginner, intermediate and advanced members, matched to the 10–14 rep range. Each weight says what it measures: the total including the bar, per dumbbell, the machine stack, or extra weight on top of your bodyweight ("BW + 10 kg"). These are averages — if you can't finish 10 clean reps, go lighter; if 14 reps feel easy, go up 2.5–5 kg.

Golden rules

Warm up

5–10 minutes of light cardio, then 2 light sets of your first exercise before the working sets.

Progressive overload

When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, add 2.5–5 % weight next session.

Tempo

Lift in about 1 second, lower in 2–3 seconds. Control the weight — don't let it drop.

Recover

Sleep 7–9 hours, eat 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight and use the rest days.
